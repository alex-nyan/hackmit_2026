# Officer capture and response coordination

Provision `PAW_PATROL_OFFICERS` on the server with JSON entries containing `id`, `name`, `badge`, and a private `passcode` (see `.env.example`). Officers log in at `/sign-in` and land on `/officer`, with capture bound to their roster ID. Accounts are administrator-provisioned, not self-registered. No roster means the existing guest demo remains available. Use one HTTPS origin for all participants and dispatch so they share media, positions, and incidents. Signed-in officers can open their workspace even if that server's default dashboard is pinned to Dispatch.

Open Camera, scan its GPS pairing QR with the phone, and grant location access. Start camera & AI listening on the Mac. macOS must already expose the iPhone through Continuity Camera; the website cannot enable it remotely. The device picker prefers the iPhone after permission reveals device labels. The panel names the actual camera, including a fallback webcam. QR links using localhost cannot be opened from another device: use the deployment's HTTPS address. A phone may need to finish GPS pairing before returning to Continuity Camera, and mobile backgrounding may suspend GPS updates.

The same panel shows other officers' footage through the existing WebRTC transport, with timestamped frame fallback. Internet peers may need TURN configured through the existing live-video settings. Closing the panel hides it; Stop or leaving the page releases capture. Browser backgrounding stops camera and audio under the existing capture policy.

Camera review waits four seconds between completed requests in standby. A significant camera hazard, distress term (including chase, pursuit, suspect, or gun), or local microphone RMS spike switches to a 500 ms wait and three-second audio clips for 30 seconds after the latest trigger. Requests remain serialized and retain failure backoff; inference latency is additional. The current audio clip completes before its shorter successor begins. This increases review frequency; it does not select a larger model or guarantee real-time detection. A spike is an amplitude change, not a gunshot classifier. Transcription and vision require the configured triage service. Without it, footage can still be shared but semantic monitoring is unavailable.

Existing server publication shares camera/audio concerns with officer, dispatch, and hospital workspaces. Spike alerts are separately published with their detector provenance and an unknown-cause label. Situation review supports operator-authored dispatch review, medic staging, and scene-access reports, plus an editable medic handoff. Confirmed details are required before publishing those actions. All reports stay in this application's shared log; no external dispatch or EMS service is contacted. The AI does not identify suspects or declare a scene safe. The checklist is a coordination aid, not an implementation of an agency-specific SOP; agency procedures and command authorization still govern response.

Verification: TypeScript checking and direct HTTP login/workspace checks with a temporary roster. Browser automation was unavailable; physical iPhone selection, microphone spike detection, and two-device streaming remain unverified. No test suite was run under hackathon mode.
